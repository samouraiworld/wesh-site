When the network goes down, phones pass the messages along.

Wesh is a free emergency communication app. When the mobile network or the Internet is cut off, phones pass messages to each other over Bluetooth, from one to the next.

Natural disaster, cyberattack, war, protests, revolutions, outages, shutdowns: Wesh keeps people nearby in touch. No account, no server, no tracking: simply peer-to-peer with the people around you, your neighbourhood, your village, your city, and why not all of Europe. Thousands of us around the world are already exploring other, autonomous ways to communicate — and it works.

Three steps

  1. Before

    Install Wesh and swap QR codes with the people close to you.

  2. During the outage

    Open Wesh. The local room brings together everyone in range.

  3. Keep it open

    Every phone relays the others' messages.

How it works

  • Public

    Local room

    Write to everyone in range who uses Wesh or BitChat. The more phones there are, the further it reaches.

  • Private

    Verified contacts

    Swap QR codes in person. Your private messages are then end-to-end encrypted.

  • Via Internet

    If you turn it on

    Off by default. Reaches verified contacts out of range, through Tor and public relays, while the Internet still works.

No phone number, no email. Your keys are created on your phone and your data stays there, encrypted. Erase all data, in settings, deletes everything after confirmation.

Good to know

The local room is public. Everyone in range can read, keep and relay what you write there.

  • It needs phones nearby. A few dozen metres, more through relays. Alone, without Internet mode, your messages wait.
  • Your phone is detectable. It broadcasts your nickname and public keys to nearby devices. These keys stay the same until you reset your identity.
  • No promise of anonymity. A nickname does not prove who someone is. In Internet mode, the Tor entry relay sees your IP address.
